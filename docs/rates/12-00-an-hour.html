How the tax is worked out

That's an effective deduction rate of 13.0%. Income Tax is 20% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270; employee National Insurance is 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270. A workplace pension (usually 5%) or a student loan would reduce the take-home figure.

What an extra shift is worth

One extra 8-hour shift a week at time-and-a-half (£18/hr)+£104 after tax (£144 gross)

Extra hours are taxed at your marginal rate, which is why the after-tax figure is lower than the gross. That's still £5,391 a year from one time-and-a-half shift a week.

Is £12 an hour good?

£12 is below the £12.71 National Living Wage for people aged 21 and over, so if you're 21+ your employer must pay at least that. It's above the £10.85 minimum for 18–20 year olds. Whether it's good depends on your hours: shift and hourly work rarely comes to exactly 37.5 hours every week, which is why the yearly figure above is only a guide.
