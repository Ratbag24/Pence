That's an effective deduction rate of 14.6%. Income Tax is 20% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270; employee National Insurance is 8% on earnings between £12,570 and £50,270. A workplace pension (usually 5%) or a student loan would reduce the take-home figure.

Extra hours are taxed at your marginal rate, which is why the after-tax figure is lower than the gross. That's still £6,065 a year from one time-and-a-half shift a week.

Is £13.50 an hour good?

£13.50 is 6% above the National Living Wage of £12.71 (21 and over, from April 2026). As a full-time salary it's roughly the same as £26,325 a year. Whether it's good depends on your hours: shift and hourly work rarely comes to exactly 37.5 hours every week, which is why the yearly figure above is only a guide.
